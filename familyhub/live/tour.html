The front desk that never misses a text

Everything the Hub's booking system does, one simple step at a time. It sits on top of Halaxy: whatever it books, moves or cancels goes straight into your Halaxy diary.

  1. Client books
  2. Gets a reminder
  3. Can't make it? Refilled
  4. Fills in intake
  5. Fees sorted

A demo with pretend clients. Nothing here is real data.

1

Clients book online, with the right person

A few quick questions match each client to the practitioner who suits them, and show real free times from Halaxy.

  • Asks who it's for (an adult, a child or a couple) and what would help
  • Shows each practitioner's fee, roughly what Medicare pays back, and their next free times
  • Books straight into the Halaxy diary, on the right appointment type
  • Clients get calendar links and can move or cancel the booking themselves
  • "Text me if something sooner comes up" puts them on the waitlist

Saves: the back-and-forth phone calls to find a time.

2

Cancellations fill themselves

When someone cancels, the Hub texts the best-matched people on the waitlist. The first person to tap gets the time, and it's written into Halaxy.

  • Matches the practitioner, day, morning or afternoon, and session length. People who've waited longest hear first
  • Only one person can get it. Everyone else gets a kind "it's been filled" text and stays on the list
  • No reply in time? The next people get it. Too close to the time? It goes back on the booking page
  • Quiet hours, an "ask me first" mode, and each practitioner can opt out

Saves: an empty $245 hour, and the phone calls down the list.

3

Reminders that free up time early

Two days before, every client gets a text with a "confirm" link and a "can't make it" link.

  • "Can't make it" cancels in Halaxy straight away, while there's still time to refill the spot
  • Shows when it stops being free to cancel
  • The dashboard lists anyone who hasn't replied, so you know who to call
  • After a month on the waitlist, people get a "still keen?" text, so offers only go to people who still want a spot

These would replace Halaxy's reminders, so clients don't get two texts.

Saves: no-shows, and confirmation calls the day before.

4

Intake forms done before day one

New clients get their form link as soon as they book, and the reminder keeps nudging until it's done.

  • Goes out automatically for first visits, referrals and people booked from the waitlist
  • Easy on a phone, and it saves as they go
  • Covers details, emergency contact, GP sharing, consent and a signature, plus a child section for child bookings
  • The practitioner sees it's done, with a "before you come" note

Happy with your Halaxy forms? Keep them and skip this part.

Saves: clipboards in the waiting room and chasing forms on the day.

5

Cancellation fees, sorted fairly

Payments and Medicare claims don't change. The Hub only sorts out the fees that come from cancellations.

  • Late cancel, but the time was refilled? The fee is waived automatically, with no awkward conversation
  • Not refilled, or a no-show? It's listed as "fee to decide". The front desk waives it, or charges it in Halaxy
  • The report shows how much the refills saved

Saves: working out who owes a fee and who doesn't.

6

One inbox for every message

Texts, emails, voicemails, missed calls and website enquiries all land in one place, already matched to the client.

  • Shows who it is, their next appointment and what they're asking about
  • Worrying messages are flagged as urgent straight away and get crisis line numbers
  • Only safe automatic replies: running late, missed call, after hours
  • Claude drafts replies to routine questions. A person always checks and sends them
  • Group texts, for example "Hannah is unwell today", which cancels her day and tells every client

Saves: checking three apps, and messages slipping through.

7

Referrals turn into bookings

GPs and midwives can refer online. Fax and email referrals are logged in the same list.

  • The client gets a text with a booking link, already set to the right practitioner
  • The Mental Health Treatment Plan stays attached to the referral
  • Each referral shows as new, invited or booked, so none get forgotten
  • The reason for referral stays with staff only

Saves: phone tag with new clients, and referrals lost in the fax pile.

8

See how the Hub is going

A dashboard for the front desk, a weekly report for you, and a page for each practitioner.

  • Dashboard: what's coming up, spots being refilled, the waitlist and settings
  • Report: bookings, cancellations, refills and dollars kept, week by week. Print it or export it
  • Practitioner view: each person's own week, new clients and intakes
  • Ask Claude: ask questions about the Hub in plain English. It never sees names
  • Booking button: one line of code adds a "Book with Hannah" button to any web page

9

Safe, private and on your terms

It was built for a health practice from the start.

Names never reach the AI

Claude sees tokens like [C1a2b] instead of names. Phone numbers, emails, notes and children's names are left out.

A person stays in charge

Only safe texts go out on their own (offers, reminders, running late). Everything else waits for a person, and every setting can be changed or turned off.

Urgent messages are flagged

Worrying words are flagged as urgent straight away, and the sender gets 000, Lifeline and PANDA numbers. Claude never drafts a reply to an urgent message.

Australian privacy law

A draft privacy page covers the Australian Privacy Principles and Victoria's Health Records Act. When live, client data is stored in Australia.

Ahpra-friendly wording

The booking pages follow Ahpra's advertising rules: no testimonials, no claims of being "specialists", and gentle wording throughout.

Written down for staff

A front-desk manual (the SOP) explains every task, from a cancellation to a sick day, so anyone can run the desk.

What going live takes

This is a demo. To run it for real:

  1. Halaxy: the practitioners in one Halaxy practice group, with Halaxy's API add-on turned on, so the Hub can see free times and book, move and cancel.
  2. Texting: a texting service with a number clients can reply to, plus forwarding the Hub's phone for missed calls and voicemail.
  3. Checks: you review the privacy page, the SOP and the wording of every text, and choose the settings (quiet hours, who gets refills, cancellation policy).
  4. A small start: begin with one or two practitioners for a month, then use the report to see how many spots were refilled.
