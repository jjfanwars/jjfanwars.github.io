The front desk that never misses a text

Here's everything the Hub's booking system does, one simple step at a time. It works with Halaxy, so bookings, cancellations and payments end up in the same place they do now.

  1. Client books
  2. Gets a reminder
  3. Fills in intake
  4. Cancels? Refilled
  5. Session paid
1

Clients book online, with the right person

A few quick questions match each client to the practitioner who suits them, and show the real next free times.

  • Asks who it's for (an adult, a child or a couple) and what would help
  • Shows each practitioner's fee, the Medicare rebate and their next free times
  • Books straight into Halaxy, on the right appointment type
  • Clients get calendar links and can move or cancel the booking themselves
  • "Text me if something sooner comes up" puts them on the waitlist

Saves: the back-and-forth phone calls to find a time.

2

Cancellations fill themselves

When someone cancels, the Hub texts the best-matched people on the waitlist. The first person to tap gets the time, booked into Halaxy.

  • Matches the practitioner, day, morning or afternoon, and session length
  • People who've waited longest hear first, and nobody gets texted too often
  • Only one person can win. Everyone else gets a kind "it's been filled" text
  • No reply in time? The next people get it. Too close to the time? It goes back on the booking page
  • Quiet hours, an "ask me first" mode, and each practitioner can opt out

Saves: an empty $245 hour, and the phone calls down the list.

3

Reminders that free up time early

Two days before, every client gets a text with "confirm" and "can't make it" links.

  • "Can't make it" cancels straight away, so there's still time to refill the spot (step 2)
  • Shows the free-cancel deadline. Late fees "may apply", but are waived if the time is refilled
  • The dashboard lists anyone who hasn't replied, so you know who to call
  • After a month on the waitlist, people get a "still keen?" text, so offers only go to people who still want a spot

Saves: no-shows, and confirmation calls the day before.

4

Intake forms done before day one

New clients get a link to a short form they can fill in on their phone. It saves as they go.

  • Details, emergency contact, GP and sharing consent, Medicare and health fund
  • A child section (parental responsibility, court orders) for child bookings
  • Card on file, telehealth location, consents and a typed signature
  • The reminder text repeats the link until the form is done
  • The practitioner reads it before the session, including a "before you come" note

Saves: clipboards in the waiting room and typing paper forms into Halaxy.

5

Getting paid without chasing

The card on file is charged when the session ends, and the Medicare claim is lodged. Receipts go out by text.

  • Optional deposits ($50 or the full fee), refunded if they cancel on time
  • A declined card gets a gentle payment link, not a phone call
  • Late cancel and no-show fees wait for you to decide, and are waived if the time is refilled
  • Refunds, receipts and a full payment history for every client

Saves: end-of-day card runs and awkward money calls.

6

One inbox for every message

Texts, emails, voicemails, missed calls and website enquiries all land in one place, already matched to the client.

  • Shows who it is, their next appointment and what they're asking about
  • Worrying messages are flagged as urgent straight away and get crisis line numbers
  • Safe automatic replies only: running late, missed call, after hours
  • Claude drafts replies to routine questions. A person always checks and sends them
  • Group texts, for example "Hannah is unwell today": cancels her day and tells every client

Saves: checking three apps, and messages slipping through.

7

Referrals turn into bookings

GPs and midwives can refer online. Fax and email referrals are logged in the same list.

  • The client gets a text with a booking link, already limited to the right practitioner
  • The Mental Health Treatment Plan is attached to the referral
  • Each referral shows as new, invited or booked, so none get forgotten
  • The reason for referral stays with staff only

Saves: phone tag with new clients, and referrals lost in the fax pile.

8

See how the Hub is going

A dashboard for the front desk, a weekly report for you, and a page for each practitioner.

  • Dashboard: what's coming up, spots being refilled, the waitlist, payments and settings
  • Report: bookings, cancellations, refills and dollars kept, week by week. Print it or export it
  • Practitioner view: each person's own week, new clients, intakes and payments
  • Ask Claude: ask questions about the Hub in plain English. It never sees names
  • Booking button: one line of code adds a "Book with Hannah" button to any web page

Saves: working out the numbers by hand.

9

Safe, private and on your terms

The system was built for a health practice from the start.

Names never reach the AI

Claude sees tokens like [C1a2b] in place of names. Phone numbers, emails, notes and children's names are left out.

A person stays in charge

Only safe automatic texts are sent on their own (offers, reminders, running late). Everything else waits for a person. Every setting can be changed or turned off.

Urgent messages are flagged

Worrying words are flagged as urgent right away, with 000, Lifeline and PANDA numbers sent. Claude never drafts a reply to an urgent message.

Australian privacy law

A draft privacy page covers the Australian Privacy Principles and Victoria's Health Records Act. When live, client data is stored in Australia.

Ahpra-friendly wording

The booking pages follow Ahpra's advertising rules: no testimonials, no claims of being "specialists", and gentle wording throughout.

Written down for staff

A front-desk manual (the SOP) explains every task, from a cancellation to a sick day, so anyone can run the desk.

What going live takes

This is a demo. To run it for real:

  1. Halaxy: the practitioners in one Halaxy practice group, with Halaxy's API turned on, so the Hub can see free times and make bookings.
  2. Texting: an SMS provider with "Surf Coast Family Hub" as the sender name, plus forwarding the Hub's number for missed calls and voicemail.
  3. Checks: you review the privacy page, the SOP and the text wording, and pick your settings (fees, quiet hours, who gets refills).
  4. A small start: begin with one or two practitioners for a month, then use the report to see how many spots were refilled.
