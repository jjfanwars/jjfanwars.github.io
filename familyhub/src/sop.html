Front desk standard operating procedure

Bookings, cancellations, the waitlist, referrals and client information · Version 0.1, November 2026 · Draft for the Hub director to review and adapt. Not legal advice.

1. Purpose and who does what

This is how the Hub handles every booking from first contact to the session, so clients get a consistent experience, cancelled time gets used, and client information stays safe. It applies to everyone who works the front desk or uses the Hub dashboard.

RoleResponsible for
Hub directorOwns this SOP and the settings (refill rules, reminders, cancellation policy). Matches “Psychology Team” referrals. Decides on exceptions and complaints. Privacy officer.
Front desk / adminRuns the daily routine. Logs referrals, chases missing plans, calls unconfirmed clients, keeps the waitlist accurate.
PractitionersKeep their Halaxy hours, session types and fees current. Choose how their cancellations are refilled. Decide whether to charge a late fee. Own their clinical records.

Halaxy is the source of truth. Every booking, wherever it starts, ends up in the practitioner’s Halaxy. The hub never keeps clinical notes.

2. Daily routine

Front desk · 15 minutes, morning and mid-afternoon
  • Open the dashboard. Clear anything under Needs you (spots waiting for approval or queued in quiet hours).
  • Referrals: action every “New” referral the same business day (section 3). Call anyone with “No booking yet” after 3 days.
  • Reminders: in the afternoon, call everyone still showing “No reply yet” for tomorrow. One call now prevents most no-shows.
  • Coming up: check for “no plan yet” tags on Medicare sessions in the next 3 days and chase the GP (section 4).
  • Glance at Activity for anything unusual, like a refill that went unfilled close to the time.
  • Check inbound texts and voicemail. Replies to hub texts that aren’t YES or STOP need a person (section 11).

3. New referrals

Referrals arrive by the online form (straight into the dashboard), fax, email, phone or letter.

  1. If it came by fax, email, phone or letter, log it with Log a referral on the dashboard. Attach the letter or plan. Then file the original as your normal process requires and delete local copies.
  2. Named practitioner: the client is texted a booking link automatically (if that setting is on). Otherwise press Send booking link.
  3. Psychology Team: the director reads the reason for referral, picks the best-matched practitioner, and presses Send booking link. Aim for within one business day.
  4. No booking after 3 days: call the client. If they can’t be reached after two calls and a text over a week, let the referrer know and close the referral.
  5. When they book, the referral and plan go to the practitioner. If the plan was “to follow”, chase it before the first session.

Urgent or risk information in a referral: don’t wait for the client to book. Tell the director or the named practitioner straight away.

4. New bookings

Most clients book themselves on the booking page. The hub picks the right Halaxy appointment type from their answers (Medicare, private, telehealth or phone), so staff don’t have to fix types.

Phone bookings

  1. Use the booking page with the client on the phone. It asks the same questions and records consent the same way.
  2. Read them the cancellation policy and confirm they agree.
  3. For new clients where a card is required, send them their booking link to add a card themselves. Never take card numbers over the phone or write them down.

Medicare and the Mental Health Treatment Plan

  • Since 1 November 2025, Better Access rebates generally need the plan and referral from the client’s MyMedicare practice or usual GP. The client or GP is responsible for the plan; we check we have it.
  • Clients can upload the plan when booking or later from their booking page. Plans that arrive by fax or email: log them against the referral and tick Plan received.
  • If a plan hasn’t arrived 2 business days before the first session, text or call the client. If it still hasn’t arrived, the session goes ahead as private unless the practitioner decides otherwise.
  • Never decide Medicare eligibility for the client. If they’re unsure, suggest they ask their GP or Medicare.

5. Reminders and confirmations

Every appointment gets one text 2 days before (setting), with a confirm link and a can’t make it link. Halaxy’s own reminders should be off for hub practitioners so clients don’t get two.

  • Confirmed: nothing to do.
  • Can’t make it: the appointment is cancelled in Halaxy and the time goes straight to the waitlist. If they tapped it outside the policy window, there’s no fee.
  • No reply by the afternoon before: call them (daily routine).

6. Cancellations and late fees

The current policy is shown to every client when they book, on their booking page and in their reminder. It’s set under Settings on the dashboard.

Free to move or cancel up to 24 hours before. Later than that, or a no-show, the policy fee applies. If we refill the time from the waitlist, there’s no fee; the hub waives it automatically.

  1. Late cancellations and no-shows that weren’t refilled show in the report with the fee the policy allows.
  2. The practitioner decides whether to charge. Use discretion for illness, emergencies, family violence, financial hardship and first-time slips, and record the reason in Halaxy.
  3. Charges are made through Halaxy’s payments, never by reading out or re-entering card details.
  4. Complaints about a fee go to the director.

7. Refilling cancelled time

When an appointment is cancelled (by the client, staff in Halaxy, or a reminder link), the hub:

  1. works out how long the gap is (up to the next booked appointment);
  2. texts the best-matched people on the waitlist: right practitioner, day, time of day and session length, longest waiting first (3 at a time by default);
  3. books the first person to tap, using Halaxy’s double-booking check so two people can never get the same time;
  4. tries the next people if nobody taps in 30 minutes, and stops 2 hours before the start. After that the time goes back on the online booking page.

If the person who got the time already had a later booking, their old time is offered on in turn.

When staff step in

  • Ask me first mode, or a practitioner set to “Ask Adi first”: approve or leave each spot from Needs you.
  • Quiet hours (8pm to 8am): offers wait until 8am.
  • A client phones to take a spot that was texted out: book it from the dashboard so the others get the “it’s gone” text.

8. The waitlist

  • Clients join online, or staff add them. Record only admin details (who, days, times, contact). No clinical notes on the waitlist.
  • After 30 days, people get a “still keen?” text. No reply within a week pauses them (they stop getting offers but stay on the list). Put someone back with one click from their record.
  • Remove people who are booked elsewhere, ask to leave or reply STOP (STOP is automatic). Their details are deleted when they leave.
  • Export only when needed, keep the file on a work device, and delete it when done.

9. Regular sessions

Clients can book the same weekday and time weekly or every 2 weeks, up to 10 sessions. If that time is taken one week, they’re offered the nearest free time that week. Practitioners can switch this off for themselves.

  • Clients can move or cancel any single session, or the rest of the series, from their booking page.
  • Medicare clients: rebates cover up to 10 sessions a calendar year under Better Access. Remind clients near the limit to talk to their GP about a review.

10. Telehealth

  • Video sessions use the practitioner’s Halaxy telehealth room. The link is in the confirmation text, the reminder and the client’s booking page.
  • Phone sessions: the practitioner calls the client’s mobile at the session time from a withheld or clinic number.
  • Practitioners follow their own telehealth procedure, including confirming identity and the client’s location at the start of each session, and having an emergency plan.
  • If a client can’t connect, call them. If it can’t be fixed in 10 minutes, offer a phone session or rebook without a fee.

11. If a message raises a safety concern

Hub texts and forms are not monitored as a crisis service, and every page says so. But if any message, form or call suggests someone may be at risk:

  1. If there’s immediate danger, call 000.
  2. Otherwise tell the client’s practitioner, or the director if they aren’t available, straight away, the same day.
  3. Give the client crisis numbers: Lifeline 13 11 14, PANDA 1300 726 306, Kids Helpline 1800 55 1800.
  4. Record what happened and what you did in the client’s Halaxy record, not in the hub.

12. Client information and privacy

The Hub handles health information under the Privacy Act 1988 (Australian Privacy Principles) and the Health Records Act 2001 (Vic). The client-facing version is the privacy page.

  • Minimum necessary: collect and look at only what you need for the task. Texts never mention the reason for a visit or the type of practitioner beyond their first name.
  • Where things live: clinical records in the practitioner’s Halaxy; bookings, waitlist and referrals in the hub (hosted in Australia when live).
  • Exports and screenshots: only on work devices, never in personal email or chat apps, and delete them when done.
  • Access and correction requests: pass to the director. Respond within 30 days.
  • Retention: practitioners keep health records at least 7 years after the last service, or until a child turns 25. Waitlist details are deleted when someone leaves the list.
  • Data breach (a lost device, a misdirected email, an export sent to the wrong person): tell the director immediately. Contain it, then assess within 30 days whether it’s likely to cause serious harm. If so, notify the affected people and the OAIC (Notifiable Data Breaches scheme).
  • Automated decisions: the hub decides automatically who on the waitlist is offered a time, and pauses people who don’t reply to check-ins. This is described in the privacy notice. If a client asks, explain how it works and review it by hand.

13. Ask Claude

The dashboard’s Ask Claude panel answers questions about bookings, the waitlist, refills and referrals. It only receives de-identified data: clients are swapped for codes, and phone numbers, emails, notes, dates of birth and referral reasons are never sent.

  • Use it for admin questions (“Which free times this fortnight suit someone on the waitlist?”), never for clinical advice.
  • Don’t type names, phone numbers or clinical details into questions.
  • Check anything important before acting on it. It can make mistakes.

15. Adding or removing a practitioner

Joining

  • Their Halaxy is in the Hub’s practice group (or their own account is connected with an API key).
  • Online-bookable hours, session types and fees set in Halaxy.
  • Ahpra registration and any endorsement checked, and their title on the website matches the register.
  • Profile, photo and “who I help” wording approved by them.
  • Their refill and regular-booking preferences set.
  • Booking button added to their own website if they have one.

Leaving or on leave

  • Mark them on leave so they disappear from booking. Existing bookings stay in Halaxy.
  • Tell everyone on the waitlist for only them, and offer other practitioners.
  • Hand over open referrals.

16. If the system is down

  • Halaxy still works on its own. Book and cancel there directly and tell clients to call.
  • Refill offers pause while the hub is down. Once it’s back, check Halaxy for cancellations made in the meantime; the hub catches up automatically, but times that are now too close need a call round.
  • Report problems to the hub provider with the time and what you saw. Don’t send client details in the report.
