# Operation Red Star: flight complications

Ideas for making the flight surprising and tense, not a checklist. Written 2026-11-06. Nothing here is built. Each one has: what happens, who handles it, and how it scales to 2 or 3 players.

## Principles
- **Two problems at once.** The best moments are when one crew member's fix makes another job harder.
- **Warn, then hit.** Every complication has a tell (a sound, a gauge, a radio line) so players feel clever, not cheated.
- **Every complication has two answers**: a fast risky one and a slow safe one.
- **Small crews:** fewer overlapping events, longer warnings, and the pilot gets assistive tools (see the end).
- **No dead time:** each phase always has one slow-burning problem running underneath the loud ones.

## Mechanical and weather
- **Icing:** wings and props ice up in cloud. Speed drops, controls get heavy. The engineer runs de-icers, but they draw power from the same bus as the arming panel. Dive to warmer air (pilot) or burn through it.
- **Headwind that changes:** the navigator announces a new wind. Fuel margin shrinks. Pilot can climb to catch a jet stream, but then the engines run hot and the cold gets worse.
- **Engine problems in tiers:** rough running (warning) -> oil leak (repair walk) -> fire (extinguish, then feather) -> dead engine. A feathered engine rattles the whole aircraft, so gunners' aim shakes.
- **Fuel crossfeed puzzle under pressure:** a leaking tank must be isolated while the pilot keeps the aircraft balanced. Wrong valve = a different tank starts draining.
- **Cabin decompression:** a flak hole near the bomb bay. Everyone gets an oxygen timer and must find a bottle; the crew nearest the hole is pulled toward it.
- **Electrical storm:** radio static and instrument flicker. The radio operator loses the code wheel for 20 seconds. Lightning can knock out a station.
- **Bomb bay hang-up:** the bay doors stick part-open at the worst moment: drag, cold, and a loose bomb swaying. A crew member must go out on the catwalk and fix it while the aircraft is rolling.

## Enemy action
- **Bandit calls from the wrong direction:** the radio reports fighters at 4 o'clock, but they come from 10 (a bad intercept). Gunners must trust their eyes over the radio.
- **A fighter that rides your blind spot:** a single ace that stays under the tail where no gun sees. The pilot has to dip a wing to expose it, which hurts the bomb run later.
- **Escort that never arrives:** the plan says fighters will cover the approach. They don't. The crew learns this by radio chatter, not a message.
- **Fighters with different habits:** one wave dives from above, one comes head-on at the nose, one stays out of range and radios positions to flak.
- **Radar lock and jamming:** a lock warning tone. The radio operator can jam, but jamming blocks decoding. The pilot can break lock by descending into the ground clutter, which costs fuel.
- **Flak walls:** bursts bracket the aircraft in patterns; the navigator can read the pattern and call "jink left on the next".
- **SAM launch:** a fast streak from the ground. Chaff (any crew member can throw it), or a hard bank, but not both.
- **Searchlights:** blind the pilot's side windows for a few seconds. The co-pilot or navigator calls the heading.
- **Ambush at the coast:** the code-word challenge is answered correctly, but the defence has the same word because the codebook was captured earlier. The radio operator notices the wrong accent/timing and has to decide to bluff or break.

## Story and crew complications
- **Mid-flight orders change:** a coded message says the target moved or the weather forces the alternate. The navigator decodes it, the pilot decides whether to trust it. Sometimes it is a trap.
- **The commissar:** pressure events. He demands the course be held "or else" while the pilot knows a detour is safer. Obeying costs fuel or time; ignoring raises suspicion and a later crew conflict.
- **A saboteur or a traitor (optional mode):** one crew member (secret) has a hidden role that degrades one system. Others must work out who from the pattern of failures.
- **Injury:** a hit knocks a crew member unconscious. The nearest player has to move them and take their station, leaving theirs empty. Medic is "whoever has the first aid kit".
- **Fatigue (long flights only):** pilot reaction slows; someone has to take the controls for 60 seconds so he can rest. Needs a second player who can fly, so it nudges everyone to learn a bit of each job.

## Set pieces (one per phase, always different)
- **Takeoff:** a cross-wind, a short runway, and an engine that surges. Abort or commit.
- **Arctic:** a whiteout. No horizon: the pilot flies on instruments while the navigator calls headings from dead reckoning.
- **Convoy sighting:** a fleet below. Optional: bomb it (fuel/risk trade) or ignore and keep the mission clean.
- **Night run:** optional lighting mode with only instrument light and red cabin lamps.
- **Final approach:** the city is lit. Bridges and skyline are the targets and the navigator picks the aim point from a checklist of landmarks.
- **Bomb run fail-states:** a flak burst jolts the sight at the last second; a fighter dives through the bomb path; one engine quits mid-run.

## Ideas that scale down
- **2 players:** the pilot has a chart and a one-button "take a fix". Events run one at a time. Gunners are AI at half skill with a visible "they need help" cue.
- **3 players:** pilot, engineer-radio, gunner-bombardier. Events overlap in pairs, never triples.
- **Assist tools for the pilot** when the navigator seat is empty: fold-down chart, a leg timer, auto-callouts of the next waypoint.

## What each role feels
- **Pilot:** decisions under pressure and responsibility for everyone else's plans.
- **Navigator:** knowledge people rely on, and the dread of being wrong.
- **Engineer:** the endless firefight with the machine; walks a lot.
- **Radio:** the one who hears bad news first and chooses how to say it.
- **Gunners:** loud, fast, simple, and the most dramatic when they miss.
- **Bombardier:** hours of waiting, then ninety seconds of absolute focus.

## Open questions
- How much of this should be random each run versus scripted? (Scripted gives set pieces; random gives replay.)
- Is a hidden traitor a fun idea for friends, or too much?
- Should a mission be allowed to end without bombing (abort/return), and does that count as a failure?

## Round 2 ideas (2026-11-06)
Built so far: fighters, injured crew, radar jamming, searchlights, leaks, bomb bay door jam, storms. More:
- **Gun jams and ammo:** a gun jams (clear it with a key) and ammo runs low. Someone carries ammo cans from the tail passage to the gunners.
- **Cabin fire:** an electrical fire starts in a compartment; extinguisher from the wall, and the smoke blinds that room. Spreads if ignored.
- **Engine fire and feathering:** the engineer pulls the right handle in time or the engine seizes. A dead engine shakes the aircraft and slows it.
- **Window frost:** windows ice up; scrape them (hold E) or the pilot and gunners lose their view.
- **Cold in the open bay:** with the door jammed, whoever works in the bomb bay slowly loses health. Treat with the first aid kit.
- **Loose bomb:** the bomb shifts on its cradle and swings with each jolt; strap it down before the bomb run or it throws off the aim.
- **Arming fault:** the arming panel throws a wiring puzzle shortly before the drop (matching coloured wires under time pressure).
- **Fuel imbalance:** the aircraft rolls to one side. The engineer transfers fuel; the pilot compensates meanwhile.
- **Trim runaway:** the controls get heavy and the pilot needs a second pair of hands on the column.
- **Spoofed radio orders:** a fake order to turn back. The radio operator checks the code word; the pilot chooses to trust it or not.
- **Compass failure and star fix:** the navigator takes a fix (a short sighting minigame) when the compass spins.
- **The ace:** one marked fighter targets a specific station (it keeps shooting the tail gun first). Kill it or lose a gunner.
- **Flak walls before the target:** timed bursts the pilot must weave through, with the navigator calling the gaps.
- **Bail-out decision:** if things go badly, the pilot can order the crew to jump. It ends the mission but lets everyone survive.
- **Morale and the commissar:** pressure events that cost time if obeyed and trust if ignored.
- **Visibility swings:** night, fog or glare at set points that change what each window shows.

## Final complication (built)

**Dead bomb electronics (Dr Strangelove):** at the end of the flight the release fails. One crewmate holds E at the bomb in the bay to climb on top and rewire it (about 10 seconds, slower if hurt, progress decays if they climb down). Sparks and jolts shake the bay and can shock the rider. When fixed the bay opens and the bomb falls with the rider on it, then the flash. The timing is a placeholder (262 s) until the bomb run phase exists: see [bomber-pacing.md](bomber-pacing.md).

The ending is now the Strangelove scene: letterboxed outside camera, the rider astride the falling bomb waving his hat over the skyline ("Yeeee-haaaw!"), a cut to a wide shot, the blast (fireball, shockwave, mushroom cloud), white-out, fade to the end card. About 16 seconds, same for the whole crew.
