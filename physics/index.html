Notes

Matched to your sheets. Toggle topic below.

1Charge & static electricity

  • Matter has protons (+, stuck in the nucleus) and electrons (−, can move).
  • Rubbing two objects together transfers electrons only from one to the other.
  • Gains electrons → negative. Loses electrons → positive. Protons are never added or removed.
  • A charged ruler picks up paper by electrostatic attraction, not magnetism.
  • Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.

Try it: Static lab.

2Current, voltage, resistance

e.g. 16 J moving 4.0 C → V = 16/4 = 4.0 V (Quiz Q6). 12 J moving 5 C → 12/5 V (Quiz Q30).

3Ohm's law & V–I graphs

V = IR. A resistor is ohmic if V ∝ I: a straight line through the origin.

  • On a V (y) vs I (x) graph, gradient = R. (Electricity 4: gradient 6/3 = 2 Ω.)
  • On an I (y) vs V (x) graph, gradient = 1/R. A steeper line means lower resistance.
  • A curved line is non-ohmic (e.g. a filament globe, which heats up so R increases).
  • Use the line of best fit, not one point, when data has an outlier (Sheet 1 Q9: 12 V reading is off).

Try it: Graph Lab.

4Series, parallel & combination

SeriesParallel
CurrentSame through every componentSplits; branch currents add to IT
VoltageShared; adds up to VBSame across every branch
ResistanceRT = R₁ + R₂ + …1/RT = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
CheckRT bigger than any oneRT smaller than the smallest

Shortcuts: n identical resistors in parallel = R/n (e.g. 3 × 90 Ω → 30 Ω). Two resistors: R = R₁R₂/(R₁+R₂).

Combination circuit method (Electricity 2, Sheet 3):

  1. Find the equivalent resistance of each parallel block.
  2. Add it to the series resistors → RT.
  3. IT = VB/RT.
  4. Series parts get IT: V = ITR.
  5. Parallel block voltage = VB − (series voltages).
  6. Each branch: I = V/R. Check the branch currents add to IT.
  7. Powers: P = VI; check they add to PT = VBIT.

If you're given a current instead of VB, work outwards from that component (Electricity 2 Q4–Q7).

5Power & energy

P = VI · P = I²R · P = V²/R · E = Pt = VIt = VQ

  • Power in watts (W) = joules per second. Time must be in seconds.
  • Blender: 1200 W on 240 V → R = V²/P = 48 Ω; I = 5 A; 12 min = 720 s → Q = 3600 C, E = 8.64×10⁵ J.
  • Most current drawn = biggest P/V (spotlight 48/12 = 4 A beats TV 180/240 = 0.75 A).

6Meters, cells, switches, globes & fuses

  • Ammeter in series (very low resistance). Voltmeter in parallel across the component (very high resistance).
  • Cells in series add voltages: three 1.5 V cells in a line = 4.5 V. Parallel cells keep 1.5 V.
  • A switch in the main line controls everything. A switch in a branch controls only that branch.
  • Globe brightness depends on its power. A globe in the main line carries IT, so it's brighter than identical globes sharing current in parallel.
  • 20 identical lamps in series on 240 V: each gets 240/20 = 12 V.
  • A fuse blows if current exceeds its rating: check I = V/RT against the fuse.

1Mass vs weight

  • Mass (kg): amount of matter. The same everywhere.
  • Weight (N): the gravitational force on a mass. Depends on where you are: Fw = mg.
  • 15 kg dog on Earth: 15 × 9.8 = 147 N. 90 kg person on the Moon: 90 × 1.6 = 144 N. 1.5 t = 1500 kg on Planet X: 1500 × 7.4 = 11 100 N.

2Newton's law of universal gravitation

F = G M m / r², with G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻². r is centre-to-centre distance in metres.

  • There is gravity between all masses: planets, stars, you and your desk.
  • Inverse square: double r → force ÷ 4. Triple r → ÷ 9.
  • Table distances are in 10⁹ m, so 57.9 → 57.9×10⁹ m. Masses are in 10²⁴ kg.
  • Earth–Mars closest approach: r = 227.9 − 149.6 = 78.3×10⁹ m.

3Gravitational field strength

g = G M / r² (N/kg), and at altitude h: r = R + h.

  • Always add the planet's radius to the altitude, and convert km → m (5000 km = 5.00×10⁶ m).
  • At 5000 km: Earth 3.08 N/kg, Moon 0.107 N/kg, Jupiter 21.6 N/kg.

5Light & light-years

  • c = 3.00×10⁸ m/s. Use s = vt with t in seconds.
  • 1 μs → 300 m. 3.4 min (204 s) → 6.12×10¹⁰ m. 2.8 days → 7.26×10¹³ m.
  • Light-year = the distance light travels in one year = 3.00×10⁸ × 31 557 600 s = 9.47×10¹⁵ m.
  • Looking far away = looking back in time: a supernova 172 ly away is seen 172 years after it happens. 172 ly = 1.63×10¹⁸ m.

6How stars live and die

A star is a balance: gravity pulls in, radiation pressure from fusion pushes out. When fuel runs out, gravity wins and the core collapses. How far it collapses depends on mass.

Nebula→Protostar→Main sequence→Red giant→Planetary nebula→White dwarf
Nebula→Protostar→Main sequence→Red supergiant→Supernova→Neutron star / black hole

Your sheet's answer to Q8 uses "less than 1.5 Suns → white dwarf; 5 Suns → black hole". Those numbers are for the leftover core, not the whole star. Check with your teacher which version they want, but always explain that the fate depends on mass.

Exam answer (model)

The fate of a star depends on its mass. When fusion stops, gravity collapses the core. Low and medium mass stars (like the Sun) swell into red giants, shed their outer layers and leave a white dwarf. Massive stars become red supergiants and explode as a supernova. If the leftover core is about 1.4–3 solar masses, it is crushed into a neutron star. If it's more than about 3 solar masses, nothing can stop the collapse and it becomes a black hole.

Static electricity lab

Electricity Quiz Q1–5: rub a plastic ruler with a woollen jumper and watch what actually moves.

Key ideas
  • Only electrons (−) move. Protons stay locked in nuclei.
  • Ruler gains electrons → negative. Jumper loses electrons → positive (it's not that protons were added).
  • The charged ruler attracts neutral paper: its electrons are pushed to the far side, leaving the near side slightly positive. That's electrostatic attraction, not magnetism.

Meters

Ammeter = Along the line (series).
Voltmeter = Visits both ends (parallel).

Don't forget to flip

1/RT = 0.2 means RT = 5 Ω, not 0.2 Ω. Always finish with ( … )⁻¹.

Power triangle family

"Power Very Important": P = VI. Swap in V = IR to get I²R and V²/R.

R + h

"Height plus home": altitude is measured from the surface, but r is from the centre. Add the radius.

Weight vs mass

Mass is matter (kg, fixed). Weight is a force (N, changes with g).

Star endings

"Wimpy, Normal-heavy, Big": White dwarf, Neutron star, Black hole as mass goes up.

Static

"Electrons Escape": only electrons transfer. Positive means electrons lost.
